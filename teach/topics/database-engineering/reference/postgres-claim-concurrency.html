Snapshot, row lock, lease token

Three different mechanisms cooperate in the Job Lease claim flow. They solve different parts of the race.

What each mechanism contributes

  • MVCC snapshot: a statement sees a committed database view; under PostgreSQL Read Committed, the snapshot is taken at statement start.
  • FOR UPDATE: locks the chosen row against competing updates/claimers until the statement transaction ends.
  • SKIP LOCKED: a competing queue consumer skips a row locked by another transaction instead of waiting; the resulting view is intentionally incomplete.
  • Lease token predicate: later renewal/completion statements require the current token, running status, and an unexpired deadline. This rejects a stale worker after takeover.

Job Lease timeline

  1. Claim statement finds a pending/expired row in its snapshot and locks the candidate.
  2. In the same statement, UPDATE writes running, a fresh token, and the expiry.
  3. The statement's implicit transaction commits; the row lock is released. The worker does its processing outside that database transaction.
  4. If the lease expires, a later claimant can replace the token. The old worker's finish predicate no longer matches.
Do not conflate them: MVCC is the visibility model; the row lock arbitrates this claim race; the token and expiry are the application's ownership/fencing check. Read Committed alone does not make a multi-statement read-then-write sequence safe.
